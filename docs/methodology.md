# Methodology

ChargeScape London answers one planning question with an auditable chain of models:

> **Given uncertain demand, where should the next public charging hubs in London go, how many ports and what power should they have, and how confident can we be in each recommendation?**

The design follows the pattern that strong operators converge on (utilisation as the target, site selection as a demand forecast, a closed loop of predict → decide → measure) while adding what public projects almost never include: explicit uncertainty, queueing, cannibalisation and a post-hoc validation plan.

## 1. Data status labels

Every dataset carries one of three labels, in the catalog (`docs/data_catalog/`) and in code:

| Label | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **observed** | a real measurement of the quantity used | OSM / Open Charge Map chargers, Census 2021, DfT vehicle licensing, DfT traffic counts, open session logs (Dundee, Perth & Kinross, Palo Alto, Boulder), Optimise Prime |
| **proxy** | a measured quantity standing in for an unmeasured one | POI density as activity attraction, share of flats as "no off-street parking", DFES forecasts |
| **synthetic** | generated by a model or assumption | Monte Carlo demand draws, literature-calibrated choice parameters |

Nothing synthetic is ever presented as observed, and the README states which results rest on which label.

## 2. Spatial framework

* Unit of analysis: H3 hexagons, resolution 8 (≈0.74 km²) for planning, resolution 9 for MAUP sensitivity.
* Census and vehicle statistics are moved from LSOA 2021 to hexagons by area-weighted interpolation (`features/grid.py`); a dasymetric refinement with building footprints is on the roadmap.
* Accessibility is measured on the OpenStreetMap drive network with igraph shortest paths (`features/accessibility.py`), giving the 2/5/10-minute logic used by practitioners instead of straight-line buffers.

## 3. Model ladder

| Level | Question | Method | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Supply audit | Where are chargers, of what power, and how does supply relate to EV ownership and housing? | three-source merge with 50 m de-duplication, Moran's I, Getis-Ord Gi*, drive-time gap analysis | `features/supply.py`, notebook 01 |
| L2 Demand surface | How much public charging energy will each hex need, now and in 2030? | structural bottom-up model with triangular priors and Monte Carlo (P10/P50/P90); taxi & private-hire layer; DFES 2030 pathways | `models/structural_demand.py`, `models/demand_pipeline.py`, notebook 02 |
| L3 Choice & queues | Which station does a driver choose, and where do queues form at the peak hour? | multinomial logit on drive times with an outside option; Erlang C per site; waits fed back into choice | `models/choice.py`, `models/queueing.py`, `models/allocation_pipeline.py`, notebook 03 |
| L4 Cannibalisation | How much of a new hub's demand is new, and how much is taken from existing sites? | incremental-demand decomposition of the logit with an outside option | `allocation_pipeline.candidate_increment`, notebook 03 |
| L5 Siting | Which candidates, under budget, hub capacity and grid headroom? | capacitated maximal covering MILP (SciPy / HiGHS) with capacity from Erlang C; greedy uncapacitated baseline | `optimize/facility.py`, `optimize/siting_pipeline.py`, notebook 04 |
| L6 Robustness | Which sites stay good across demand scenarios? | a plan per scenario (P10/P50/P90), each evaluated exactly under the others with a fixed-plan LP: expected coverage, minimax regret and a robust core. The single two-stage robust MILP is implemented and tested but does not close its gap on the London instance, so it is not used for results | `optimize/facility.py`, `optimize/siting_pipeline.py`, notebook 04 |
| L7 Sensitivity | What moves when behavioural and service assumptions change? | re-solve at £20M on a fixed candidate set for 9 changed assumptions; exact and same-or-adjacent-hex overlap with the base | `optimize/sensitivity.py`, notebook 04 |
| L8 Transfer test | Do context features explain observed utilisation in cities that publish sessions, and does that transfer? | LightGBM on station context; random vs leave-city-out CV; scoring of London sites | `features/station_context.py`, `models/ml.py`, notebook 05 |
| L9 Delivery | How does a planner use this? | MapLibre GL maps (offline-capable), HTML slides with live maps, PPTX backup, key-numbers file that feeds the README | `viz/export_maplibre.py`, `scripts/`, `docs/` |

## 4. Structural demand priors (L2)

| Parameter | Prior (low, mode, high) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Annual km per car | 9,000 / 11,500 / 14,000 | UK National Travel Survey average car mileage ≈ 7,400 miles/yr; London cars drive less than the national average |
| kWh per km | 0.16 / 0.19 / 0.24 | EV Database real-world consumption for popular UK models, plus a winter/heating penalty |
| Public share of energy, home charging available | 0.05 / 0.12 / 0.25 | Survey evidence that owners with a driveway charge mostly at home |
| Public share of energy, no off-street parking | 0.55 / 0.75 / 0.95 | Drivers without home charging depend on public/on-street charging; the TfL 2025 strategy models this group explicitly |
| Rapid share of public energy | 0.25 / 0.40 / 0.60 | UK network energy split between slow/fast on-street and rapid/ultra-rapid hubs (Zapmap / TfL utilisation by type) |
| PHEV electric-km factor | 0.25 / 0.40 / 0.55 | Real-world PHEV utility-factor studies (ICCT) |

The priors are deliberately wide. Calibration targets are aggregate observed quantities: total public energy implied by DfT/Zapmap charger counts and TfL-reported utilisation by charger type, and per-session kWh from open session logs. The model card in `docs/model_card.md` lists which priors dominate which component; notebook 04 reports how the siting result moves when the behavioural and service assumptions change.

## 5. Validation strategy

1. **Spatial cross-validation** (leave-group-out: city, borough or H3 parent) for any fitted model; random K-fold is reported only as a contrast to show its optimism.
2. **Calibration to aggregates**: modelled city-wide energy vs implied energy from observed supply × observed utilisation.
3. **Transfer test**: models trained on cities with observed sessions, scored out-of-city.
4. **Post-hoc plan**: the README states exactly which observed quantity (utilisation of chargers opened after the analysis date, from future DfT/Zapmap releases) would confirm or refute each recommendation. This is the "predict → deploy → measure" loop that makes the system a decision tool rather than a map.

## 6. What the London run showed (September 2026)

* **Calibration.** Modelled public demand 285 GWh/yr (P10 to P90: 216 to 361) against about 760 GWh/yr that the network *could* deliver at TfL-observed occupancy; the rapid-type component implies about 13 sessions per rapid charger per day, matching TfL's 34% occupancy with 38-minute sessions. The near-home component is well below the slow network's theoretical throughput, consistent with long-dwell, low-charging-time on-street sessions.
* **Queues, not distance.** Median drive time to a rapid site is 2.2 min, yet 348 of 632 rapid sites have P(wait) > 0.5 at the peak hour while their 24-hour utilisation has a median of 15%.
* **Cannibalisation.** A hub placed at the hex with the largest unmet demand takes 64 to 75% of its sessions from existing sites, more for larger and faster hubs.
* **Regime.** With P(wait) ≤ 15% at the peak hour a 4 × 150 kW hub absorbs ≈33 sessions/day; unmet or queue-constrained rapid demand is ≈2,686 sessions/day. Siting is capacity-bound: £20M buys 51 hubs serving 63% of it, £40M buys 98 hubs serving 86%. Greedy uncapacitated coverage overstates served demand by up to 4.7× at small budgets.
* **Demand uncertainty.** A plan is optimised for each of P10, P50 and P90 demand and evaluated exactly under the others. At £5M to £20M the plan optimised for *pessimistic* (P10) demand has zero regret in every scenario: capacity-bound hubs placed where even low demand is dense fill up whatever happens, while hubs placed for median demand may not. At £40M, when capacity no longer binds everywhere, the P90 plan is best. The three plans agree on a robust core of 22 of 51 hubs at £20M (same or adjacent hex).
* **Assumptions.** Across 9 changed behavioural and service assumptions, a median 75% of the £20M hubs stay in the same or an adjacent hex; the service level moves coverage the most.
* **Transfer.** Context features explain observed utilisation poorly out of city (leave-city-out R² -0.25 vs 0.70 with random folds). Any site model must be validated out-of-city against usage.

## 7. Known limitations

* No open London-specific session data at charger level; observed behaviour comes from other UK cities and from fleet trials (Optimise Prime, Low Carbon London) and is used for parameters, not for training a London target.
* Supply positions come from Open Charge Map, OpenStreetMap and a 2021 registry snapshot; ports missing from all three are represented by borough-level scaling to DfT totals, not by exact positions.
* Area-weighted interpolation assumes uniform density inside LSOAs.
* Choice-model parameters are literature-informed until revealed-preference data are available; notebook 04 reports how the siting result changes with them.
* Grid capacity is represented by proxies (substation headroom where UKPN publishes it); a real connection study is outside scope.
